A window is a plan.

Outside exists for the small decision between checking the weather and actually getting out the door. Daily highs and a rain icon hide the useful hours in between.

This is a noncommercial experiment, built around one transparent rule: your whole outing should fit your limits.

How your window is found

We check every possible 1–3 hour block between 07:00 and 20:00. Any hour outside your rain, wind or feels-like limits removes that block. Missing data and thunderstorm codes also remove it.

Remaining blocks are ranked by lowest peak rain chance, then lowest peak wind, then average distance from 20°C. Ties go to the earlier time. Rain chance is an hourly forecast, not the probability of staying dry for the whole outing.

What this can—and cannot—tell you

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo. This is for ordinary urban outings. It does not assess route conditions, air quality, UV, lightning proximity or official warnings. Your limits are preferences, not safety thresholds. Check local warnings before leaving.

Is the planner doing its job?

The logic is checked against an independent exhaustive search. Evaluation results have not loaded.

These checks measure scheduling correctness on synthetic inputs, not real-world forecast accuracy or proven user benefit.
